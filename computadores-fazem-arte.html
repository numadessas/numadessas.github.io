01 · IDENTITY

The visual system.

The palette of fluorescent pink, purple, lilac and lime yellow isn't just decorative, it's mine. It's the same chromatic language present in my own artwork, so bringing these colors into the project is a natural extension of my identity. Beyond that personal signature, it gave the project a distinct visual identity and let me approach technology with artistic sensibility and a social lens, moving away from the cold, usual aesthetic of the crypto world.

02 · GRAPHIC MATERIAL

Teaching, designed.

I wanted something organic and full of movement, expressive and impossible to ignore. I brought in fluid shapes, hand-drawn lettering, generous spacing and playful infographics that turn complex ideas into something people can absorb easily. The design is part of the pedagogy, not just packaging for it.

2.3

The seed-phrase notebook

Editorial · Handmade · Product

I created a handmade notebook to gather seed phrases in one place. People usually write these phrases down anywhere, often on loose scraps of paper. Hardware wallets come with a small sheet, but almost no one has an organized physical spot dedicated to all their wallets. I first made it for the students I worked with during my master's. Today I also make them as gifts for people I meet at in-person events. Turning something abstract and intimidating into an object you can hold in your hands and find beautiful.

04 · IN PRACTICE

From page
to classroom.

I've always been involved with social movements and education. I was already running educational and community actions long before I entered the blockchain world. My research is the evolution of what I already did: using education as a tool for autonomy and care. Blockchain is the subject I'm immersed in right now, but my commitment to making knowledge accessible has always been the same.

Made with the community

Built alongside activist-educators Débora Aguiar and Manu Marques, the course paired blockchain fundamentals with lessons on decoloniality and financial education — and brought in trans, Black, and non-binary NFT artists to make the field feel close, not distant.

With academic guidance from João Marcelo Teixeira (master's advisor) and Leonardo Castillo (who invited Numa to teach Design para Web3 at UFPE), and the scientific-initiation students who researched alongside the project — Milena Gomes (Pedagogy), Vitória Farias (Design), and Artur Couto (Computer Science, in memoriam).

Jun 2023 — Mar 2024
Pilot classes
Seven experimental sessions — from a graffiti mutirão with VR headsets to design-school lectures — testing pedagogy and shaping the material before it existed as a finished object.
Mar — Aug 2024
Scientific initiation
Three undergraduates from Computer Science, Design, and Pedagogy validated the material, 3D-scanned paper sculptures, and minted their own NFTs on 3.land — later co-authoring a paper at XR4EDU / IMX 2025.
Jul — Sep 2024
The free course
33 applications, eight in-person lessons, meals and printed material provided. A warmly received introduction to Web3 with critical debate at its center.
2024 — 2025
Beyond the classroom
The material travelled: WebMedia 2024 minicourse, NFT Brasil at the São Paulo Biennial, IslandDAO in Koh Samui (Thailand), and an intensive at UFSJ — each adapting the same visual system.

Design here is not decoration — it is mediation. A warmer, more organic language became the bridge between complex technology and the people it too often excludes.
